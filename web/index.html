Proposed analog: Médano Blanco (Sechura, Piura) ↔ Bagnold Dunes (Gale crater, Mars). Both are active barchan dune fields shaped by steady wind. Bagnold was the first active dune field studied in situ on another planet (Curiosity, 2015–2017).

What makes the analogy strong

Same landform and process: active barchans and linear dunes built by a persistent wind regime, loose sand that traps wheels, slipfaces and metre-scale ripples. Measured migration on both bodies (Sentinel-2, MINAM; HiRISE).

Where it fails

Composition (quartz vs basalt–olivine), atmosphere (1 bar vs 6 mbar: Mars sand moves rarely), gravity, no vegetation on Mars. Médano Blanco is a protected tourism site with use restrictions.

How it helps exploration

A field for rover mobility and sand-trap recovery drills (the Spirit failure mode), dune-crossing path planning, dust and bedform change-detection methods, and for calibrating orbital measurements against ground truth, 50 km from Piura.

Honest framing. The analogy is morphological and process-based (barchans, steady wind, loose sand), not mineralogical: Bagnold sand is basaltic and olivine-rich, Médano Blanco is quartz sand (INGEMMET unit Q-eo). Gale is a rover science site, so the pitch is training field for mobility and operations in sand that any Mars base will face. Why it matters for analogue sites

Comparing an Earth site with Mars only works at matched scale. Knowing each camera field of view and pixels per metre tells you which Earth photos (drone, ground, Sentinel-2 cut-outs) are like for like with a Perseverance frame.

EDR and RDR products

EDR (Experiment Data Record) is the raw image as downlinked; RDR (Reduced Data Record) is calibrated or derived: colour, stereo anaglyphs, disparity and range maps, meshes and mosaics.

Reading a filename

The first two letters are the instrument, the next one is a colour flag (E raw Bayer, M monochrome), then come timestamps, the rover site and drive, and the product type.

What ALTERGEA does

It ranks Earth locations by how closely their environment resembles the conditions expected at permanent Moon and Mars base sites, to help choose training fields on Earth. The flagship candidate is the Médano Blanco dune field (Sechura, Piura) as an analog of the Bagnold Dunes (Gale crater).

Data used

  • NASA POWER: precipitation (PRECTOTCORR), daily temperature range (T2M_RANGE), surface albedo (ALLSKY_SRF_ALB) and 10 m wind, mean and max (WS10M, WS10M_MAX), climatological means. Precomputed for the 14 base sites; fetched live for sites you add.
  • Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (ESA) via Microsoft Planetary Computer (STAC API with public signing, no account): altitude, mean slope at 30 m and detrended roughness over a ~3 km window. This is the slope the model uses, at the resolution the Mars side (HiRISE DTM resampled to 30 m) can match. Precomputed for the base sites.
  • SRTM (NASA/NGA) elevation, ASTER GDEM (NASA/METI) fallback for polar sites, via OpenTopoData: altitude and a regional slope over a ~4 km window (5×5 grid, ~1 km spacing); used for sites you add here.
  • NASA Mars Trek WMTS tiles: Viking MDIM 2.1, MOLA colour hillshade (463 m), MRO CTX Gale mosaic, MRO HiRISE Curiosity roving-site mosaic, HiRISE 1 m DEM of Gale.
  • NASA GIBS / Worldview: MODIS Terra, VIIRS NOAA-21 and VIIRS SNPP true-colour tiles as Earth overlays, with a date control in the side panel. Open NASA Worldview.
  • NASA Earthdata Search: catalogue of NASA Earth-observation datasets. The CMR API is queried live for the collections and granules (MODIS, Harmonized Landsat/Sentinel-2) that cover each site. Open the Sechura search.
  • NASA PDS Geosciences Node, Mars Exploration Program (MSL Curiosity, MER Spirit and Opportunity, Mars 2020): the Orbital Data Explorer is queried live for HiRISE DTMs and images around each rover site (Mars twin tab). Open the portal.
  • Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A via Planetary Computer (Earth Search on AWS as fallback catalogue): cloud-free scenes 2017–2026 to measure the dune's migration by masked cross-correlation (see the Mars twin tab).
  • INGEMMET GEOCATMIN: integrated 1:100k geology and geomorphology, as WMS overlays and point queries.
  • MINAM / SINIA (2022): Médano Blanco diagnostic: dune type, area, migration 2006–2022, management.

Limitations

  • NASA POWER cells are ~50 km: values describe regional climate, not an exact point.
  • Slope is a regional mean from ~1 km spacing, not metre-scale terrain. Earth–Mars slope or roughness comparisons must use matched resolution.
  • The Médano Blanco ↔ Bagnold analogy is morphological and process-based, not mineralogical.
  • Thresholds and weights are documented assumptions. Next step: calibrate with Mars (MOLA, MEDA/REMS) and Moon (LOLA, Diviner) reference sites, and add INGEMMET lithology to the score.
